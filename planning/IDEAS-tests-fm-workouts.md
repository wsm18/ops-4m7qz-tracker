## 1. "Make the tests more like games, train what they test, and give dopamine spikes on level-up"

> *"for the tests make it so that they are more like games that test each thing, except for the memory unless if you can make a game version of that too, make tests for all possible tests, even the academic ones, make them fun to do instead of a test, and make them so that the games train what the test is testing for along with testing for it, make training fun so that people want to do it, give dopamine spikes when you increase your skill level"*

**First-pass answer (below) was wrong — Wyatt's correction:** I initially assessed the 8 cognitive drills in `test.js` (tap-on-green reaction time, adaptive digit-span, n-back, go/no-go, etc.) as already satisfying "test = game." **He disagrees.** His actual bar: *"I dont agree that the cognitive tests are game like, I want more genuine games inbedded into it instead of progressively incrasing tests, I want it to test it and train it without the person noticing what its doing for the test and training, and them to just select what they want to focus on for that session and to have genuine fun."*

That's a real, specific distinction — **gamified testing vs. stealth assessment**:
- What exists now: the real psychometric task (react-to-green, recall-the-sequence, tap-only-on-green), wrapped in a timer/score UI, with difficulty that adapts trial-to-trial. It's honest and functional, but it still *reads* as "I am being measured," just with a nicer UI than a lab task. A staircase that gets harder every trial is a test procedure wearing a costume, not a game someone would choose to play for its own sake.
- What he wants: an actual game — something with its own theme, goal, and moment-to-moment fun that a person would enjoy independent of what it's secretly measuring — where the *mechanic* happens to require the same underlying cognitive demand (fast reaction, holding a sequence, filtering distractors, rapid symbol-matching, arithmetic), so playing it well and playing it *at all* both produce the training and the measurement as a side effect. The person shouldn't be able to point at a moment and say "that was the test part."
- Session flow he's asking for: don't present "Test 4: N-Back" — present a menu of **what to focus on today** (reflexes, memory, focus/filtering, processing speed, math, reading), pick one, and get dropped into a genuinely fun game for that construct. Score/level mapping happens invisibly underneath, same as today's `scoreToLevel`, but nothing during play should look like a clinical readout.

**Corrected roadmap direction** (concrete game concepts per construct, disguising the same underlying measurement `test.js` already computes):
- **Reaction time** → a real reflex game (e.g. whack-a-mole / dodge-and-catch with a theme) where hit-timing *is* the reaction-time data, but the player sees "score" and "combo," not "trial 3 of 5, 312ms."
- **Digit span / working memory** → reframe as something like a safe-cracking or heist-code game — recall-the-sequence is the mechanic, but it's dressed as cracking a lock, not "repeat the digits."
- **N-back** → an arcade "spot the repeat" game (e.g. sorting incoming signals/packages, flag the one that matches N-back) rather than a bare grid + MATCH button.
- **Go/No-Go** → a game built entirely around *not* hitting the wrong target under pressure (a themed impulse-control game), not a circle-tapping accuracy drill.
- **Processing speed** (symbol→number) → a fast-paced match/sort puzzle game, escalating speed as a game-feel thing (juice, combo multiplier), not a labeled "60s sprint."
- **Mental math sprint** → an arcade math game (e.g. slice/select the correct answer among decoys as they move/appear) instead of a typed-answer timer.
- **Reading speed/comprehension** → hardest one to disguise fully, but a mystery/detective framing (read the clue fast, comprehension check = "what do you do next" plot choice) could make comprehension feel like progressing a story, not answering a quiz.
- **Academic quizzes** (`QUIZ_BANK`, currently plain multiple-choice, no game framing at all) — same principle applies here too, and it's still the most under-built area: needs its own game skin, not just a combo-meter bolted onto multiple choice.
- **Memory (SRS/palace):** he left room for "unless you can make a game version of that too" — worth a real attempt at a game skin for flashcard/loci review, not an assumed exception. Don't rule it out by default.
- **Dopamine spikes:** still valid as stated — wire `showLevelUp()` (or a game-appropriate variant, e.g. an in-game level-clear celebration) to fire on skill-level-tier crossings and PRs, not just a toast.

**Size:** this is now a bigger, more creative lift than "gamify existing tests" — it's closer to designing N small original games, each one validated to still produce a usable, honest measurement of its construct (measurability is a hard rule — a game can't be so abstracted from the construct that the score stops meaning what it claims to mean). Worth treating as its own multi-session workstream, prototyping one construct first (reaction time is probably the easiest real-game wrapper) before committing to all of them.

**Follow-up clarifications (resolved):**
- **Game concepts:** when asked whether the sketched concepts above (whack-a-mole reaction time, safe-cracking digit span, signal-sorting n-back, impulse-control go/no-go, slice-the-answer math) land, Wyatt said **he wants to brainstorm more first** — none of these are locked in. Treat the list above as a first-pass idea dump to react to and expand on, not a chosen design. A future session should spend real time generating/comparing alternatives per construct before committing to any one concept.
- **Raw stats vs. fully hidden:** resolved — **show the real measured number/stats after the session ends** (results screen, history, sparklines — same honesty/measurability treatment every other skill gets), but **hide everything clinical during play itself**. So the "invisible" property applies to the moment-to-moment play experience only, not to the historical record — this keeps the app's no-faked-metrics value intact while still meeting the stealth-during-play bar.
- **Academic/knowledge quizzes — stealth bar resolved:** asked whether quiz content (inherently recall-based — you either know the fact or you don't) needs the same full-disguise treatment as the cognitive drills, or whether game-feel scoring is enough given the content itself is unavoidably visible as "answering a question." **Wyatt's answer, verbatim: *"what about making a simple game where there is a character that you control and in order to progress in the game and reach the goal you have to use the knowledge of the quiz?"*** This resolves it toward full disguise, not just a combo meter: a controllable character progressing toward a goal, where **advancing requires correctly applying `QUIZ_BANK` knowledge** — e.g. the right choice/action/path at a given point depends on knowing the actual ROTC content, so answering correctly *is* how you move the character forward rather than a separate multiple-choice screen interrupting play. This is the same "the mechanic requires the construct" principle as the cognitive-drill concepts above, just applied to knowledge recall instead of a psychometric task — worth designing as its own concept alongside (not instead of) the per-construct cognitive games, still subject to the "brainstorm more before locking in" note above.

### 1a. Cognitive-drill game concept brainstorm (dedicated design pass, per Wyatt's request)

Per his correction, none of the original one-per-construct sketches are locked in. Below is a real brainstorm — 2 candidate concepts per construct where a genuine second option exists, each evaluated for (a) does the game stand on its own merits independent of what it measures, (b) does the mechanic still require the actual construct so the measurement stays honest, (c) thematic fit with the app's military/Yggdrasil identity. A recommended pick is marked, but nothing here is final — flag anything that doesn't land and it gets reworked.

- **Reaction time** (replaces tap-on-green):
  - **Sentry** *(recommended)* — you're on night watch; silhouettes flash briefly at the treeline, tap the instant a threat silhouette appears (vs. a decoy/non-threat that must be ignored). Reaction latency on true-threat taps is the data; the visible feedback is a "threats spotted" tally and a night getting progressively tenser, not a stopwatch. Thematically on-brand (sentry/guard duty is real ROTC content) and doubles as light impulse-control practice since decoys punish over-eager tapping.
  - **Quick Draw** — a duel ladder against escalating AI opponents; a "draw" cue fires at a random delay, first to react wins the round. Win streak = escalating opponent speed. Strong standalone arcade fun, less thematically specific than Sentry but a clean fallback if Sentry's night-watch framing doesn't land.
- **Digit span / working memory** (replaces flash-sequence + typed recall):
  - **Land Nav Relay** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: a small stylized course map shows numbered waypoint markers. A sequence of them lights up one at a time (waypoint 3, then 7, then 2, then 9 — exactly today's digit-flash, just shown as map points instead of numerals). Once the sequence finishes, you tap the same waypoints back in order on the map to "relay the route to a follower." Get it right, next round adds one more waypoint to the sequence (same adaptive staircase behavior test.js already has). The only real change from today is the skin — numbers become map points, "repeat the digits" becomes "relay the route" — but that skin plus the visual map (reusing map/path visual language the app already has elsewhere) is what makes it feel like a small navigation task instead of a memory drill.
  - **Vault Crack** — the originally-sketched safe-cracking version (dial sequence to reproduce). Kept as an alternate: has a punchier "click" moment of success than Land Nav Relay, but the heist trope doesn't tie to ROTC content the way Land Nav does.
- **Typing speed** (replaces type-the-sentence WPM test): typing itself is already a well-trodden fun genre, so this is more about picking the skin.
  - **Comms Relay** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: short "radio transmission" text fragments scroll onto the screen one at a time (or queue up), each with a short countdown before it "garbles"/scrolls away. You type each one out as fast and accurately as you can before it's lost — same keystroke-timing-based WPM/accuracy math as today's sentence-typing test, just delivered as a stream of short urgent messages instead of one long sentence, with a small "message decoded" confirmation per line instead of a running WPM counter visible mid-play.
  - **Convoy Run** — typing accuracy/speed controls a vehicle's speed down a route (classic typing-race genre). Purely arcade, no specific thematic tie — a fine fallback, less distinctive than Comms Relay.
- **N-back** (replaces grid + MATCH button):
  - **Perimeter Watch** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: a row of watch-post icons is shown; one lights up at a time in a continuous rotating sequence. You tap a button whenever the currently-lit post is the same one that was lit N steps back (N selectable 1–4, same as today) — mechanically identical to n-back, just visualized as tracking a patrol/watch rotation instead of a bare grid with a "MATCH" button.
  - **Signal Sorter** — incoming signals/packages arrive continuously; flag the one matching N-back. Logistics-themed alternate, functionally identical mechanic to Perimeter Watch, different skin.
- **Go/No-Go** (replaces tap-green-avoid-red):
  - **Fire Discipline** *(confirmed — comfort check passed)* — silhouettes flash (hostile vs. friendly/non-combatant), tap (fire) only on hostile, hold on friendly. Wyatt confirmed the framing is good ("Good, keep it") — abstract silhouettes, tasteful register, maps the accuracy/impulse-control construct onto a real, legitimate ROTC training concept (target discrimination). This is now the settled Go/No-Go concept, not just a recommendation.
  - **Checkpoint** — wave through friendly vehicles at a checkpoint, flag suspicious ones. Same underlying mechanic; kept only as a fallback skin, not needed given Fire Discipline is confirmed.
- **Processing speed** (replaces symbol→number matching pad):
  - **Cipher Desk** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: a cipher key (symbol → number mapping) is displayed, and a stream of symbols appears that you convert to the matching number on a keypad as fast as possible — same underlying matching-pad mechanic and 60-second-sprint timing as today's test, just visually framed as decoding a codebook under time pressure, with "message decrypted" feedback instead of a bare running score.
  - **Manifest Check** — match incoming crate/cargo symbols to the correct bin number. Logistics-themed alternate, same mechanic.
- **Mental math sprint** (replaces typed-answer arithmetic sprint):
  - **Fire Mission** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: you're given a target offset (e.g. "add 200, left 50") and must quickly compute the corrected values and enter them — same rapid-arithmetic-under-time-pressure mechanic as today's sprint, but feedback is a simple hit/near-miss/miss visual showing how close your correction landed, instead of a bare running score. Real ROTC-adjacent procedure (call-for-fire adjustment genuinely uses quick mental math), and "how close did it land" is a more satisfying per-answer feedback loop than a number ticking up.
  - **Resupply Count** — quickly compute rationing/quantity math for a supply drop. Logistics-themed alternate.
- **Reading speed/comprehension** (replaces timed passage + self-reported comprehension check) — also fixes a real weakness: today's comprehension check is self-reported, which is a softer measurement than the app's other tests.
  - **Intel Briefing** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: you read a short "intercepted report" fast (same timed-passage mechanic as today), then instead of rating your own comprehension, you're given 2–3 possible next actions and must pick the one that's actually consistent with what the report said. Whether you pick correctly *is* the comprehension measurement — an objective decision-correctness signal replacing self-report, which is an honest measurement upgrade, not just a game skin.
- **Academic/knowledge quizzes** — fleshing out Wyatt's "controllable character" answer into something concrete:
  - **Climb the Tree** *(recommended)* — moment-to-moment: a small character icon sits on the app's own Yggdrasil tree art and needs to move from one branch/node toward a goal further up. At each junction there's an obstacle (a locked branch, a gap to cross) passable only by picking the correct action from a couple of options — and which option is "correct" is determined by real `QUIZ_BANK` knowledge (e.g. the right land-nav technique, the right first-aid step, whatever the current topic covers). A wrong pick isn't a hard fail, just a small setback (slide back one node, quick retry) before you can try the junction again. This ties the quiz-game directly into the app's existing tree/world visual identity instead of inventing an unrelated setting.

**All concepts confirmed** after reviewing the fuller moment-to-moment descriptions above — Land Nav Relay, Comms Relay, Perimeter Watch, Cipher Desk, Fire Mission, Intel Briefing, and Climb the Tree are all locked in alongside the already-confirmed Fire Discipline. **Still open:** which construct to actually prototype first once a build is greenlit, and the session-start "pick a focus" UI question.

## 2. "Make the FM plan use my actual gym-access schedule, and put the right sessions on gym days"

> *"there are times when I have access to gyms on a schedule but those might not be the optimal days to have it. make the FM training plan take the schedule of days I have access to the gym and make them optimal for training... why have a run on days I have access to the gym, why not have the gym day be used for things like the mock AFT instead"*

**What's already built (checked `src/core/training.js`, `src/tabs/plan.js`):** `WEEK_PLAN` is a **fixed Sun–Sat calendar** — each calendar day is hard-mapped to a session type (s1 Lower+Push, s2 Run, s3 Upper+Core, s4 "AFT Circuit", s5 Mobility) regardless of equipment. `S.hasGym` exists, but it's a single global on/off switch that swaps *exercise lists* within whatever session already landed on that day (`sessionEx()` picks `.gym` vs `.bw` per exercise) — it does not move *which session type* falls on which day. So today, if your gym access happens to land on a run day, you get a bodyweight-flavored run day and the gym sits unused for anything but incidental extra work.

**What's genuinely new here:** the schedule needs to invert — instead of "day → fixed session type → equipment-aware exercise list," it should be "user declares which days they have gym access this week → the planner assigns session *types* to days, putting equipment-dependent sessions (heavy lower/push lifting, the AFT circuit / mock AFT) on gym days and equipment-free sessions (runs, mobility, bodyweight upper/core) on non-gym days." This is a real logic change to how `WEEK_PLAN` gets resolved, not a data tweak — `WEEK_PLAN` stops being a static object and becomes a function of a declared weekly gym-access pattern, still respecting the existing session rotation/intensity/recovery rules (adaptive missed-session tracking, recovery advisory) underneath.

**Answer / recommendation:** yes, this is worth doing, and it directly answers your own "why have a run on a gym day" question — no reason to, once the planner knows your access pattern. Concretely:
- Add a weekly gym-access input (which of the 7 days you'll have gym access this week — a simple per-day toggle, re-enterable weekly since access is often irregular per your own framing).
- Reorder session-type assignment so gym-requiring sessions (heavy lower/push, AFT circuit/mock-AFT) land on declared gym days first, then fill remaining days with the equipment-free sessions (runs, bodyweight upper/core, mobility) — preserving the existing weekly volume/rotation/recovery logic, just re-sequenced.
- The "mock AFT on a gym day instead of a run" idea folds directly into this — see idea 2b below, since AFT currently has no guided practice-taking flow at all.

**Same-day/ad-hoc changes (resolved):** asked whether the planner only needs a weekly upfront gym-access declaration, or also needs to support unplanned same-day changes (e.g. unexpected gym access today) — Wyatt confirmed **support same-day ad-hoc changes too**. So this can't just be a once-a-week form that locks in the week's session-type assignment — the re-planning logic needs to be callable live, the same day, and re-suggest a different session type on the spot if today's actual access differs from what was declared. That likely means the "which session type goes today" decision should be re-derivable at render time from current-day gym status, not computed once at week-start and cached.

**Size:** medium-large — touches `WEEK_PLAN`, `coach-today`, and the weekly render in `plan.js`. Real architecture change but bounded to one tab + one core module.

### 2a. Gym-access input design (proposal — resolves the still-open weekly-vs-recurring question)

Rather than treating "weekly declaration" and "same-day override" (already confirmed required) as competing designs, propose a hybrid that gets both:
1. **A saved default weekly pattern** — set once (e.g. "I usually have gym access Mon/Wed/Fri"), so most weeks need zero re-entry. This is the "remember a recurring pattern" half of the original question.
2. **A per-week confirm-or-adjust step** — the first time you open the plan tab each week, it shows the default pattern and lets you adjust just that week's days before it locks in (covers irregular weeks without discarding the convenience of a default).
3. **A live same-day override, always available** — a simple "actually I do/don't have gym today" toggle on the current day, independent of steps 1–2, which re-runs the session-type assignment for just today on the spot (this is the piece already confirmed required last round).

This satisfies "re-enterable weekly" and "supports same-day ad-hoc changes" simultaneously without picking one at the expense of the other — the default pattern minimizes friction, the weekly confirm step catches irregular weeks, and the live override handles same-day surprises.

**Confirmed:** all three layers (saved default pattern + weekly confirm-or-adjust + live same-day override) match what Wyatt wants — proceed with the full design as proposed.

### 2b. Mock/practice AFT flow (implied by "gym day → mock AFT instead of a run")

**What's already built (checked `src/tabs/aft.js`, `src/core/aft-scoring.js`):** AFT support is deep but is a **manual score-entry log**, not something you "take" in-app — you do the real event and type in the result afterward. There's no timed/guided walkthrough of the 5 AFT events, no in-app run timer or rep counter for a mock attempt.
**Answer:** building a guided "take a mock AFT" mode (walks the 5 events in order, times/counts as you go, feeds straight into the existing `aftSave()`/scoring pipeline you already trust) is genuinely new work, but it reuses all the scoring math already built — you'd only be building the administering UI, not the scoring. This is a natural gym-day session type to slot into idea #2's rescheduling.

**Cadence (resolved):** asked whether a full 5-event mock AFT should happen every gym day, occasionally, or split into single-event practice — Wyatt chose **let the coach decide adaptively based on the training cycle**, no fixed cadence. So this isn't a static rule (e.g. "monthly") to hardcode — it plugs into the same adaptive logic that already exists for missed-session tracking and recovery advisory (`trackMissedSessions`, `getAdaptiveNote`, `renderRecoveryAdvisory`): full mock AFT, single-event AFT practice, and normal heavy lifting are all options the coach can choose between for a given gym day, based on where you are in the training cycle (how recently tested, how loaded/recovered, how close to an actual test date, etc.) — not a fixed rotation.

## 3. "Let me input what equipment I have, and use the most optimal exercises even if unorthodox/invented"

> *"why not make the FM tab take in an input of what equipment I have access to in order to make optimal exercise use for that day. also why not make it so that the FM tab has the most optimal exercises even if they are unorthodox or have to be invented in order to work every muscle that we want"*

**What's already built:** equipment-awareness today is exactly two states — `hasGym` on/off — plus a weather-driven indoor/outdoor swap. There is no granular equipment list (barbell vs. dumbbells vs. bands vs. pull-up bar vs. machines), so a day with "just a pull-up bar and bands" and a day with "full commercial gym" currently look identical (both just "gym mode").

**Answer / recommendation:** this is a real, worthwhile expansion, and it composes with idea #2 rather than competing with it:
- Replace the boolean `hasGym` with a small equipment inventory (checkboxes: barbell, dumbbells, bands, pull-up bar, machines, kettlebell, etc.) — re-enterable per session or per week, however often access actually varies for you.

- On "invented/unorthodox exercises where needed": worth doing, but only where a real gap exists (a muscle/movement pattern your equipment can't hit with a standard exercise) — not as a blanket replacement for standard, well-validated movements. Each invented exercise needs the same honest how-to/safety treatment every other exercise gets (per `CLAUDE.md` rule #2 — no faked/unverifiable content, so "invented" still means "described precisely enough to execute safely," not vague).

**Approval flow for invented exercises (resolved):** asked whether invented/unorthodox exercises need Wyatt's explicit review before shipping, or should auto-generate trusted to the safety bar — he chose **auto-generate, trust the safety bar**, no separate approval gate. So an invented exercise is held to exactly the same honest, precisely-described how-to/safety standard as every other exercise in the app (same bar `EX_HOWTO` content already meets), but doesn't need a manual review step before it goes live in a session.

**Equipment profiles (resolved):** asked whether one editable equipment inventory is enough, or whether it's worth saving multiple named profiles for meaningfully different setups (e.g. dorm gym vs. main gym vs. home) — Wyatt chose **multiple saved profiles**. So the equipment inventory isn't a single global list to edit in place each time; it should support saving a few named profiles (exact names/count TBD — "Main gym," "Dorm," "Home" were suggested as examples, not a final list) that get picked between at session start, similar in spirit to picking gym/no-gym at the start of a card-game session (idea #5).

### 3a. Equipment taxonomy + starter presets (proposal)

To make the inventory concrete rather than an open-ended list, propose a small fixed set of equipment tags (deliberately coarse — tagging every individual machine would be unmaintainable, and the exercise library already only distinguishes bodyweight vs. gym):

- **Bodyweight helpers:** pull-up bar, dip bars/parallettes, gymnastic rings
- **Portable/bands:** resistance bands, suspension trainer (TRX-style)
- **Machines:** one umbrella tag ("machines available") rather than per-machine granularity, since `BEGINNER_RX`/session lists don't currently name specific machines either
- **Cardio equipment:** rower, stationary bike, treadmill — relevant mainly for swapping the run-day session when outdoor running isn't an option (composes with the existing `WEATHER` indoor/outdoor swap)

And propose **starter presets** so you're not building a profile from a blank list: **"Full Gym"** (everything above), **"Minimal / Dorm"** (pull-up bar + bands only), **"Bodyweight Only"** (nothing checked) — as editable starting points you'd rename/adjust to your actual real access, not fixed categories you're stuck with.

**Resolved — real profiles, not generic presets:** Wyatt's actual situation is **two distinct real profiles**: **"ROTC/Campus Gym"** (full/near-full gym equipment — barbell, dumbbells, machines, etc., all tags from the taxonomy above available) and **"Dorm"** (bodyweight-only/minimal — no equipment tags checked, matching today's existing bodyweight exercise lists). This replaces the earlier generic "Full Gym / Minimal-Dorm / Bodyweight Only" three-preset guess with his real two-situation setup — the tag taxonomy above still stands as the underlying vocabulary each profile is built from, but only these two named profiles are needed, not three generic placeholders.

**Size:** large — this is effectively a data-model change to the exercise library (equipment + muscle tags on every exercise) plus a new selection algorithm. Should probably be scoped as its own multi-session `IMPROVEMENTS-vNNN.md`, similar in spirit to the skills pyramid work — real content authoring, not a quick pass.

## 5. "Turn workouts into a card game — cards drawn per set, suits = exercises, numbers = reps, thresholds to advance"

> *"making it so that each exercise in a workout is replaced with 4 for each of that exercise, where there are rounds of card drawings(sets) and the card number represents the number of that exercise you have to do and then the suit represents the exercise and there is a conversion rate between them and then to move on to the next exercise group of that workout you have to reach a certain number of total of the highest worth exercise"*

Originally flagged by Wyatt as not-fully-sold. He's since added a much more concrete spec (below), but on being asked directly, said to **hold this in planning, not build yet** — so this section is now a fuller spec, still unbuilt, still awaiting a build greenlight.

**Relevant precedent already in the app:** this isn't a foreign mechanic for Operations — `src/tabs/today.js`'s "Today's Hand" already renders skills as literal suited cards (`SK_SUIT`, `.th-card`) drawn via a deterministic daily shuffle. A workout-card mechanic would extend an existing visual/mechanical language rather than inventing a new one from scratch, which fits the "symbolism is intentional" rule better than most novel ideas would.

**How the mechanic reads, mechanically:** each exercise slot in a session gets 4 suited variants (e.g., harder/easier regressions, or 4 distinct movement patterns for that muscle group). Each set = a card draw: rank → rep/rounds count for whichever suit's variant it lands on, with a conversion rate so a "3 of clubs" and a "3 of hearts" aren't necessarily equal effective load if the variants differ in difficulty. A block ends when a running total of the *highest-value* suit's reps crosses a threshold, at which point you move to the next exercise group.

**Resolved architecture question — coach prescribes, game mode presents (Wyatt's own framing, verbatim):** *"the coach gives the material to the game of what needs to be worked and the game makes it into a game, someone can choose if they want the more variety option from the game mode, but the coach gives an initial exercise muscle group to work and the game mode picks exercises to work those."* Concretely: `coach-today`'s existing prescription logic (which muscle groups/session type today is, per `WEEK_PLAN`/`BEGINNER_RX`) stays the authority on *what* needs to be worked — the card game does not replace or override that decision. Card-game mode is a **selectable alternate front-end** that takes the coach's muscle-group/session target as input and picks/sequences specific exercises (respecting gym/no-gym, and eventually the equipment inventory from idea #3) to satisfy it via the draw-and-threshold mechanic, instead of just handing you the fixed prescribed list. **Detailed session-flow spec (Wyatt, verbatim intent, restated precisely):**

### 5a. Deck/suit/rank/threshold design pass (worked math, per Wyatt's request)

**The core problem to solve:** a "standard" skin (4 suits × ranks 1–13) and a "skill-derived" skin (potentially dozens of suits, each with its own rank ceiling — a skill with an 8-level ladder tops out at rank 8, one with 5 tops out at rank 5) can't both feed a flat "rank = reps" rule, or a top card means something different depending which skin is active. Two structural problems need solving separately: (1) how a draw resolves to *which exercise variant* when suit count varies, and (2) how a draw resolves to *how many reps*, fairly, when rank ceilings vary.

**1. Suit → exercise-variant resolution (works the same regardless of skin):** every exercise slot still has exactly 4 real variants (per the original "4 for each exercise" spec) — that doesn't change based on deck skin. What changes is only how a drawn suit maps onto those 4 variants:
- **Standard skin:** 1:1 — 4 suits, 4 variants, direct mapping (♠→variant 1, ♥→variant 2, etc.), same as originally sketched.
- **Tarot / fully-random skins:** same 1:1 idea, just with different suit labels/art — still 4 "families" worth of cards, purely cosmetic re-skin, no math changes.
- **Skill-derived skin (many suits):** suits get bucketed into the same 4 variant-groups (e.g. round-robin assignment, or grouped by which of the 4 variants each skill's theme is closest to) — so a draw of *any* skill-suit still resolves to one of the 4 real variants underneath. This keeps the auto-advance/threshold logic identical no matter which skin is active; only the visual richness of what gets drawn changes.

**2. Rank → reps, normalized (fair across uneven rank ceilings):** instead of `reps = rank` (which breaks the moment two suits have different rank ceilings), use a ratio: `reps_this_draw = round((rank / suit's own rank ceiling) × variant's max-rep scale)`. A top card in *any* suit — whether its ceiling is 5 or 13 — always means "this variant's full prescribed rep count," and a low card always means proportionally fewer reps. This is the piece that makes the skill-derived deck's uneven rank ranges actually fair instead of accidentally over- or under-loading whichever suit happens to have a smaller ceiling.

**3. Conversion rate + "highest-value suit" threshold, concretely:** each of the 4 variants gets a **value multiplier** reflecting relative difficulty (e.g. hardest variant = 1.0, easier regressions = 0.5–0.75). Define a running `progress` total for the current exercise group:
`progress += reps_this_draw × (this_variant's_value_multiplier / highest_variant's_value_multiplier)`
So a draw of the hardest variant counts fully toward the threshold; a draw of an easier variant counts proportionally less (this *is* the "conversion rate between them" from the original spec, made concrete). The group advances once `progress` **meets or exceeds** a fixed target threshold (a per-exercise-group constant, tuned like today's `BEGINNER_RX` rep targets) — matching the auto-advance-on-exceed rule already specified.

**4. Difficulty rating — confirmed adaptive:** the per-draw difficulty rating **adjusts future draws, it's not just logged**. Confirmed as designed: a "too easy" rating nudges the next draw's odds toward higher ranks/harder variants for that exercise slot; "too hard" nudges toward lower ranks/easier variants — a weighted-draw bias, still fully automatic (doesn't violate "the app auto-draws the card").

**Confirmed:** the suit-bucketing approach (point 1) and the value-multiplier/weighted-progress/threshold formula (point 3) both match Wyatt's intent as designed — no changes needed to either.

**5. Progressive-overload guardrails — decided (Wyatt asked me to reason it through and commit, not just guess and re-ask):**
- **Anchor the threshold to today's real prescription, not an arbitrary new number:** set each exercise group's threshold equal to the total reps `BEGINNER_RX`/`prescriptionFor` already prescribes for that muscle group (e.g. if today's prescription is 3×12=36 reps, the group's card-game threshold is 36 "highest-value-equivalent reps"). This guarantees volume parity with the existing, already-trusted prescription system by construction, and the card game's job is to make hitting that same number fun, not to quietly change how much work is prescribed.
- **Asymmetric bounds, because overshoot and undershoot don't carry the same risk for someone training themselves up on a beginner program:**
  - **Overshoot — bound tightly.** Exceeding a beginner-appropriate prescribed volume risks excess fatigue/joint stress accumulating across a session (a full session touches multiple exercise groups, and `BEGINNER_RX` is written for beginners, not an auto-regulated advanced lifter who can safely absorb a large overrun). Cap any single draw's contribution at **~12% of the threshold** (~4 reps on a 36-rep threshold). Since the mechanic advances as soon as `progress` meets/exceeds the threshold, this also mechanically bounds the *fastest* possible completion to roughly 8–9 draws — no single draw, including the one that crosses the threshold, can overrun by more than that ~12%.
  - **Undershoot — bound more leniently.** A slow grind costs time/motivation, not safety, so it can tolerate a looser bound. Set the minimum-average-draw floor at **~8% of the threshold** (~3 reps on a 36-rep threshold), bounding worst-case grind length to roughly 12–13 draws.
  - **Net effect:** any group completes in roughly 8–13 draws — in the neighborhood of "a few sets," just broken into smaller, more frequent feedback moments than today's 3-big-sets structure, which fits the game's more frequent dopamine-loop design from idea #1's spirit — while total workload stays close to (and never wildly exceeds) today's real `BEGINNER_RX` prescription.
- **The adaptive difficulty-bias mechanism (already confirmed) reinforces this automatically** — a real unlucky/lucky streak mid-group gets corrected by the same too-easy/too-hard adaptive nudging already agreed on, not just by the fixed percentage bounds.

**Status: design pass complete, still not greenlit for a build session** — Wyatt chose to keep this in planning when asked directly, and confirmed the section 5a math otherwise. The earlier recommendation to prototype a single exercise slot before building the full mechanic across every session still stands as the suggested next step whenever this gets greenlit; the guardrail-tuning work above (point 5) would need to happen as part of that first prototype, not deferred further.

Fits the honesty/measurability value tightly (states only what's true in the logged data) and stays fully offline (all computation local, no new data collection).

### B. Whole-tree "smart focus" recommender — "what actually matters this week, out of 3800+ skills"
Confirmed gap: Today's Hand (`today.js`) only sorts by decay urgency (`skDaysLeft()`) and cycles daily — there's no engine that looks at the *entire* tree and identifies the single highest-leverage thing to work on right now relative to a stated goal (commissioning readiness, Cyber branch). With the tree now past 3800 skills, "browse and decide what matters" is a real, worsening scale problem. A recommender that weighs current levels/decay/mastery-gaps against milestone/goal weighting and surfaces "this is your actual bottleneck right now, here's why" would turn an unmanageably large tree into one clear weekly focus — directly useful, not just more gamification.

### C. AAR-style structured reflection journal — "a real Army practice, not another game layer"
Confirmed gap: the existing "Counseling log" (`records.js`, `S.counseling`) stores unstructured free-text `{date, type, people, summary, plan}` — genuinely different from a real After-Action Review, which has a specific, well-established structure (what was supposed to happen / what actually happened / why / sustain vs. improve). A lightweight AAR journal — promptable after a bad AFT test, a broken streak, or a rough week — would reinforce a real skill the user will need as a commissioned officer (structured self-reflection), not just add another gamification mechanic. Strong fit with the app's honesty value and ROTC authenticity; distinct in kind from everything else in this doc, which is all games/scheduling.

### D. Unified cross-tab timeline — "one view of everything coming up"
Confirmed gap: quests have `due` dates, bosses have `targetDate`, AFT entries have test dates, awards/events are mostly historical — each tab manages its own dates in total isolation (`board.js` tasks don't even have dates at all), with no merged "what's coming up across everything" view anywhere in the app. Lower on the novelty scale than A–C (this is aggregation, not new analytical capability) but genuinely useful and comparatively cheap to build — reduces real cognitive overhead of checking 5 tabs to know what's due when, likely surfaced as a new section on the Today tab.

**Relative ambition, for context:** A and C are the most novel (genuinely new analytical/behavioral capability, nothing like them exists anywhere in the app); B is novel in scope (whole-tree reasoning) but builds on existing decay/urgency infrastructure; D is the most modest (pure aggregation of data that already exists, no new logic).
